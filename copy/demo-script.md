# FlockCast: 3-minute demo script

Total: 3:00. One person drives the laptop, one speaks. Open http://localhost:8000/app/ before you start.

## 0:00–0:40 The concern
"San Francisco has about 400 Flock license-plate cameras. Say one reads your plate on the way to work. California law says that read can go to California police, and nobody else. No out-of-state police, no federal agencies.
So where does it actually go? We followed one plate read, step by step, using only public documents."

## 0:40–2:15 It working on real data
*(Point at the stat cards.)*
"Here's the number. SFPD's own audit found **299 searches** of SF's network, from May 2025 to May 2026, run for federal and out-of-state agencies through a regional fusion center. The Attorney General reads state law as not allowing that."

*(Click hop 1, Camera → Flock.)* "Your plate goes to Flock's cloud. That's legal: state law says hosting doesn't count as sharing."

*(Click hop 3, SFPD → NCRIC.)* "SFPD shares access with NCRIC, a fusion center. NCRIC's own policy says California police only. Here's the quote, and here's the source link. This is where the 299 searches came through."

*(Click hop 4, out-of-state.)* "For about six months, Flock's Nationwide Lookup left SF's data open to out-of-state police. SFPD says Flock turned it on without telling them. The SF Standard counted more than 1.6 million out-of-state searches."

*(Click hop 5, federal.)* "Federal agencies too: the DEA, IRS and US Marshals, according to SFPD."

"Every one of the five steps has at least one documented incident. Every claim links to its source, so don't trust us, click it."

## 2:15–2:50 One honest limit
"We can only see what SFPD, NCRIC and Flock chose to publish or audit. Search logs aren't public, so anything nobody audited is invisible. And we say 'possible conflict', not 'violation'. We're not a court."

## 2:50–3:00 Who it's for, and next
"The Board of Supervisors votes on extending Flock's contract in February. This is for them, and for anyone whose plate gets read. Next: neighborhood-level counts and a policy-versus-promise table for every SF department."
