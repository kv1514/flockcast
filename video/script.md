# FlockCast: video script (about 80 seconds)

Two parts. Part 1 is the two of you on camera. Part 2 is a voice-over on top of `video/flockcast-demo.mp4`, which was recorded from the live site and is timed to these lines.

Live site: https://kv1514.github.io/flockcast/

This is the script that matches the pre-recorded `flockcast-demo.mp4` (no clicking needed). `copy/video-script-map.md` is the other version, for recording the site live with the ▶ button. Use one, not both.

---

## Part 1: On camera, the problem (about 25 seconds)

**Kausthubh:** Every time you drive in San Francisco, a camera can read your license plate. SFPD runs about 400 of them, made by a company called Flock.

**Helen:** California law says those reads can only go to California agencies. But SFPD's own audit found 299 searches run for federal and out-of-state agencies.

**Kausthubh:** So we asked one question: where does a single plate read actually go, and is each place it lands allowed to have it?

---

## Part 2: Screen recording with voice-over (about 55 seconds)

Play `video/flockcast-demo.mp4` and read each line when its stop appears. The times match the video.

| Time | On screen | Voice-over |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | FlockCast opens on San Francisco, districts shaded | **Helen:** This is FlockCast. We follow one plate read across a real map, using only public records. |
| 0:06 | The plate flies to Atlanta: **Stop 1, Flock Safety cloud** | **Kausthubh:** Stop one: Flock's cloud. A private company in Atlanta holds your read. The rule allows that, but only as SFPD's host. |
| 0:15 | The plate flies back to SF: **Stop 2, SFPD Headquarters** | **Helen:** Stop two: SFPD. Officers can search it, but every search needs a case number or a stated reason. |
| 0:24 | Short hop across SF: **Stop 3, NCRIC** | **Kausthubh:** Stop three: NCRIC, a regional fusion center. Allowed, as a California agency. But this is where those 299 searches came through. |
| 0:33 | Lines fan out across the country: **Stop 4, Other states** | **Helen:** Stop four: other states. The rule says not allowed. Yet for about six months, Flock left San Francisco's data open to police nationwide. |
| 0:42 | The plate flies to Washington: **Stop 5, Federal agencies** | **Kausthubh:** Stop five: federal agencies. Also not allowed. The record shows a possible conflict here too. |
| 0:51 | The whole path, coast to coast | **Helen:** Every claim links to its source. One limit: we only see what was published or audited. The Board of Supervisors votes on Flock's contract in February. This is for them, and for you. |

Ends at about 0:58.

---

## Say only these numbers

About 400 cameras · 299 searches (May 2025 to May 2026) · about six months (Aug 2024 to Feb 2025) · February vote.

Say "possible conflict", never "violation" or "illegal". Never mention where any camera is.

## If you run long

Cut the last two sentences of Part 1's second line ("But SFPD's own audit…") only if you keep the 299 line at Stop 3. Or drop "using only public records" at 0:00.
